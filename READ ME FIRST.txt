RIDGELINE - find where a landscape photo was taken from its skyline
=====================================================================

WHAT IT DOES
  * Reads the skyline (the line where land meets sky) in a colour or black-and-white photo.
  * Asks you where you think the photo was taken, then works out the real skyline seen from
    thousands of places around that spot, using open elevation data (out to 120 km, with the
    curvature of the Earth and the bending of light by the air), as in Afterglow.
  * Compares the photo's skyline with each of them in every compass direction, and shows the
    best-fitting places on a map with the direction the camera was facing, how much it was
    tilted, and how wide the lens was.
  * Draws the skyline the elevation model predicts on top of your photo, so you can see for
    yourself whether the match is real.

FIRST START (Windows)
  1. Python must be installed once: https://www.python.org/downloads/
     On the first installer screen tick "Add python.exe to PATH".
  2. Double-click "Start app.bat".
     The first time it installs the app's parts (2-5 minutes, needs internet).
  3. The app opens in its own window (address http://localhost:8512).
     Keep the small black window open while you use the app; close it to stop the app.
  4. At the end of the first-time setup a Ridgeline icon is put on your Desktop and in the Start
     menu. If it is missing (or you move the folder), double-click "Create desktop icon.bat".
  5. Phones and tablets: open the Ridgeline launcher page (see the GitHub instructions) and choose
     "Add to Home Screen" for a Ridgeline icon that opens the online app.

USING IT
  1. Choose a photo: "From this device" (on a phone this opens the photo library), or
     "From Google Drive": paste the link of a folder or picture that is shared as
     "Anyone with the link" (Share > General access), press "Open link" and pick a picture.
  2. Say where the search should start: type a town, valley, park, peak or coordinates, or tap
     the map. Choose how far around it to search (2-30 km). Larger areas take longer.
  3. Check the orange skyline line on the photo. Use the tools above the photo:
       Draw skyline - drag along the true skyline where the line is wrong;
       Not skyline  - drag sideways across trees, buildings, poles or clouds that hide the hills;
       Is skyline   - use a stretch again;  Undo.
     Then say how wide the view is. Camera data from the original photo is best; if the picture
     was cropped or you do not know, choose "Not known" and the app tries 20 to 90 degrees.
  4. Press "Search for the place". A search of 10-12 km around a point takes about 2-3 minutes.
  5. Results: a verdict (Strong / Possible / Unsure), a heat map of how well each place fits,
     numbered best places (tap one), the view cone, the photo with the model's skyline drawn on
     it (blue dashes) and a panorama. "Download these places (CSV)" saves the list.

GOOD TO KNOW
  * Photos with distinctive hills, cliffs or mountains work best. Flat land, a sea horizon or a
    view blocked by trees cannot be placed.
  * A valley view whose skyline is very close (under 1-2 km) changes quickly as you move, so use
    a finer grid spacing (150-200 m) for such photos.
  * The elevation data does not include trees, buildings or snow cornices; small differences
    along the ridge are normal. Mark tree-covered stretches as "Not skyline" for a better fit.
  * The photo must be the right way up and not a stitched panorama.
  * "Strong" means the best place fits at least 1.5 times better than any place more than 1 km
    away; in testing every such result was right. "Possible" (1.15-1.5 times) was right about
    half the time: check the blue model line on the photo. "Unsure" means several places fit
    about equally: clean up the skyline, give the lens angle, or search a smaller area.
  * Needs an internet connection (elevation data, maps and place search come from the internet).
    Elevation tiles are kept in the "cache" folder, so a second search in the same area is faster.
  * Your photo stays on this computer (or, for the online version, on the app's server for the
    length of your visit); it is not sent anywhere else.

DATA
  * Elevation: AWS Terrain Tiles (SRTM, USGS 3DEP, GMTED2010, ETOPO1 and national models).
  * Place search: Open-Meteo geocoding (GeoNames). Maps: OpenTopoMap, OpenStreetMap, Esri.
  * Google Drive: Google's public folder view and download addresses (no sign-in).
